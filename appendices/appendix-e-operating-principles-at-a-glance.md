# Appendix E — Operating Principles at a Glance

**Before acting.** What is the actual business problem? What evidence do we have? What remains uncertain? What capability is at risk? What dependencies are involved? Who owns the capability? Who has authority to decide?

**Before changing.** What are we protecting? What is the blast radius? What is the expected result? What would make us stop? How will we verify success? How will we recover? Is the organization ready to operate the resulting state?

**During incidents.** Protect what already works. Do not make an unstable platform more complex. Preserve evidence. Reduce blast radius. Distinguish symptoms from causes. Prefer controlled degradation to uncontrolled instability when the business trade-off supports it. Stop when continuing creates more risk than it removes.

**During evolution.** Understand the current state. Define the direction. Compare the risk of changing with the risk of staying. Stay as close to upstream as practical. Remove unnecessary divergence. Use lifecycle events to simplify. Ensure the organization can operate the target state. Measure whether control improved after the transition.

**For the organization.** Build teams, not collections of experts. Distribute critical knowledge. Make ownership visible. Separate ownership from accountability. Make decision rights explicit. Keep organizational boundaries from becoming operational boundaries. Treat sustainable workload as a reliability concern. Ensure external expertise leaves internal capability behind.
